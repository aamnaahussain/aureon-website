Good to know

Why security needs attention now

As more of the Kingdom’s business moves online, opportunities for attackers grow with it. Ransomware groups run like businesses, phishing emails are now written by AI, and staff work from home, from phones and from cloud apps that sit outside the office firewall.

Regulators have responded. The NCA, SAMA and SDAIA have all published detailed requirements, and customers increasingly ask suppliers to prove they are secure before signing.

FAQ

Common questions

Regulatory statements are general summaries; always confirm against the current published frameworks.

Where should a smaller business start?

With five basics: multi-factor authentication on email, up-to-date endpoint protection, regular patching, tested backups kept offline or off-site, and staff awareness training. These stop the large majority of common attacks. An assessment shows which you are missing.

What is the difference between a vulnerability assessment and a penetration test?

A vulnerability assessment scans your systems and lists known weaknesses. A penetration test goes further: a tester tries to exploit them, as an attacker would, to show what could really be reached. Most organizations benefit from both.

Do the NCA controls apply to us?

The Essential Cybersecurity Controls are mandatory for government entities and for private organizations that own or operate critical national infrastructure. Many other companies adopt them because customers or regulators expect it. We can help you confirm what applies.

How often should we test our security?

At least once a year, and after any major change such as a new application, a cloud migration or an office move. Regulated sectors often require more.

What happens if we are attacked?

Speed matters most. With an incident response plan, your team knows whom to call, what to isolate and what to preserve. We help you prepare the plan in advance and support you during an incident.

Do you know how exposed you are?

A security assessment answers that in two to three weeks: a clear report, a prioritized plan and an honest conversation about what to do first.
